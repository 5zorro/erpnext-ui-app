Pay Outstanding — how the flow attaches to the table

Section 1 compares flow shapes on one invoice with 8 installments batched into two payments — the only variable is how the middle columns connect many schedule rows to one aggregate row. Section 2 takes the chosen shape (B) and puts it under both sorts on a harder case: 3 invoices × 3 installments, batched one per invoice.

  • Aggregate rows are as tall as the rows they draw from — 5 members = 5 row heights. Sizing them by amount instead (what production does today) gave a 4-row-tall suggested payment fed by 5 payments, and a 3-payment group taller than a 5-payment one. 5zorro, 2026-09-08: “if this mockup is to try to be production, it should look sane.”
  • The brace covers the bundle, not the row — bracketing an 8-row cell when the flow occupies its top fifth implies there is something to catch in the other four fifths.
  • The brace has its own gutter — it used to sit on top of the strands it was pointing at.
  • The label sits where the bundle is, per option.
